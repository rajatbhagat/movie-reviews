## The short version

Part One built the world; Part Two finally has a story to tell in it. It is
enormous, patient, and far more willing to sit with the ugliness of what Paul
becomes. See it on the biggest screen you can find.

## What worked

The scale never feels like spectacle for its own sake — the sandworm riding
sequence works because the film spent two movies teaching you why it matters.
Villeneuve keeps the frame uncluttered, so the size of things registers.

The tonal shift is the real achievement. This is a movie about a prophecy being
weaponised, and it never lets Paul off the hook for going along with it.

## What didn't

A couple of political threads get compressed into single scenes, and the
Harkonnen material occasionally tips from menacing into theatrical. Chani is
given the film's most interesting position and slightly too little room to
argue it.

## Final thoughts

Sits comfortably with the best of modern big-budget science fiction, and it
manages the trick most middle-of-trilogy films fail at: it is the reason the
first one was worth making.
