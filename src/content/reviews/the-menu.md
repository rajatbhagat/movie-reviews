## The short version

A tasting menu at an unreachably exclusive restaurant goes exactly as badly as
the trailer implies. Sharp and mean while it is still making fun of people;
weaker once it has to resolve anything. Worth it for Ralph Fiennes.

## What worked

Fiennes plays the chef as a man who has been complimented into madness, and
every course announcement is funnier than the last. The production design sells
the whole joke before anyone speaks.

Anya Taylor-Joy's outsider is the right anchor — she is the only character
allowed to find the situation absurd.

## What didn't

The third act trades satire for mechanics, and the mechanics are ordinary. The
film has a lot to say about the people who consume art and much less about the
people who make it, which is the half I wanted.

## Final thoughts

A great two-thirds of a movie. I would rewatch it up to the cheeseburger and
happily stop there.
